# Fictional Client Mandate

## Client

**Harbourstone Foundation** is a fictional UK institutional investor managing a **£100 million balanced-growth portfolio**. It exists only for this educational project.

**Learning checkpoint:** The complete investment mandate—including the client profile, investment objective, risk tolerance, spending and liquidity policy, and strategic constraints—was understood and approved by Yusuf on 10 August 2026. The asset universe remains provisional until separately taught, tested and approved.

## Investment problem

The investment committee needs a liquid strategic allocation that can pursue long-term real capital growth without depending excessively on one asset, one market regime or one optimisation output.

## Objective

Seek to outperform UK CPI inflation by approximately 3% per year, annualised over rolling five-year periods, before transaction costs and tax.

This is an aspiration, not a guarantee. The core project will not optimise directly against a CPI series, so achievement of the objective will be assessed as historical context rather than presented as a forecast.

**Approval checkpoint:** Yusuf verified that the inflation-linked objective protects purchasing power, correctly calculated CPI +3%, and explained why the target is an objective rather than a guarantee on 10 August 2026.

## Horizon and risk profile

- Investment horizon: at least five years
- Risk profile: moderate
- Drawdown reference: approximately 15–20%
- Key concerns: equity crashes, inflation shocks, rapid interest-rate increases, widening credit spreads, rising crisis correlations, currency exposure and insufficient liquidity

The drawdown figure is a decision reference rather than a guaranteed loss limit. A portfolio that remained within this range historically could experience a larger future loss.

**Approval checkpoint:** Yusuf correctly calculated an 18% drawdown, explained why a 20% loss requires more than a 20% gain to recover, and distinguished moderate risk from loss-free investing on 10 August 2026.

## Spending and liquidity

Harbourstone plans to distribute approximately 3% of the portfolio annually to charitable causes, initially about £3 million. If the CPI +3% objective is achieved, the real-growth component could approximately support this spending while the CPI component preserves purchasing power over time, before fees and costs. This remains an objective rather than a guarantee.

The next 12 months of planned grants should be held in cash or readily saleable investments so Harbourstone can pay charities on time without being forced to sell difficult-to-trade or depressed assets. The investable universe is therefore restricted to publicly traded index funds or exchange-traded product proxies; illiquid private equity and direct property are excluded from the core project.

The core model assumes the portfolio can be rebalanced monthly under normal market conditions. Public trading does not guarantee perfect liquidity during a crisis, and the model does not capture bid–ask spreads, market impact, suspended trading or stressed-market liquidity. Historical investment performance will be analysed without deducting actual charitable withdrawals, which is a stated modelling limitation.

**Approval checkpoint:** Yusuf correctly linked the additional 3% return objective to the 3% spending policy and explained why Harbourstone needs 12 months of readily available grant funding on 10 August 2026.

## Strategic constraints

1. Long only.
2. No leverage.
3. No short selling.
4. Weights must sum to 100%.
5. Each individual asset weight must be between 0% and 40%.
6. Mandate-constrained portfolios must allocate at least 30% in total to UK gilts, GBP-hedged global aggregate bonds and sterling investment-grade corporate bonds.
7. The recommended portfolio must contain more than one independent source of return and must explain any deliberate overlap.
8. Tax, transaction costs, fees beyond those embedded in adjusted product prices and market impact are excluded from the core analysis.
9. Historical estimates and optimisation results are evidence, not forecasts.

The equal-weight and transparent 60/40 portfolios are comparison benchmarks. If a comparison benchmark does not satisfy a recommendation-specific constraint, that exception will be labelled rather than hidden.

**Approval checkpoint:** Yusuf explained long-only and no leverage, identified the 40% single-asset cap, verified that 10% + 12% + 8% meets the 30% fixed-income minimum, connected the constraints to diversification and moderate risk, and explained why historical optimisation may not hold in the future on 10 August 2026. He also distinguished sterling corporate bonds—defined by GBP issuance—from bonds issued only by British companies.

## Portfolio decision principles

The final recommendation must:

- connect each allocation to the mandate;
- balance growth, defence and inflation sensitivity;
- consider drawdown, tail loss, stress behaviour, risk contribution and concentration;
- be understandable and implementable;
- acknowledge overlapping exposures and hidden currency risk;
- avoid treating the highest historical return or Sharpe ratio as automatically best; and
- state conditions under which the recommendation may fail.

## Review policy

The strategic allocation is assumed to be rebalanced monthly for the historical simulation. In a real mandate, the committee would also define tolerance bands, cash-flow rules, governance responsibilities and an explicit review calendar. These operational details are outside the core model and will be identified as limitations.
