# Fictional client mandate

## Client and objective

Harbourstone Foundation is a fictional UK charitable investor managing a £100 million balanced-growth portfolio. It seeks UK CPI +3% annualised over rolling five-year periods, before external transaction costs and tax. The objective is aspirational and cannot guarantee a positive return in any year.

The core model does not ingest CPI. It therefore evaluates portfolio structure and risk but does not claim that the real-return objective has been achieved.

## Horizon and risk

- Investment horizon: at least five years
- Risk profile: moderate
- Drawdown reference: approximately 15–20%
- Main risks: equity losses, inflation, rate increases, wider credit spreads, rising crisis correlations, currency exposure and impaired liquidity

The drawdown range guides comparison rather than imposing a guaranteed loss limit. Future losses can exceed historical or simulated drawdowns.

## Spending and liquidity

Harbourstone plans to distribute about 3% of the portfolio each year, initially £3 million. It should retain the next 12 months of planned grants in cash or readily saleable assets. The core universe therefore uses publicly traded products and excludes private equity and direct property.

Monthly rebalancing is assumed under normal market conditions. Public listing does not guarantee liquidity during a crisis, and the model omits bid–ask spreads, market impact, trading suspensions and charitable withdrawals.

## Strategic constraints

1. Long only, with no leverage or short selling.
2. Portfolio weights must sum to 100%.
3. Each asset must remain between 0% and 40%.
4. IGLT, AGBP and SLXX must total at least 30%.
5. The recommendation must explain overlap and contain more than one source of return.
6. Historical and synthetic estimates are evidence about the model, not forecasts.

## Decision principles

The committee should compare growth, total and downside risk, drawdown, tail loss, stress behaviour, contribution to return and contribution to risk. It should not select the highest return or optimiser score automatically.

Any implementation decision would also require product-cost analysis, a cash-reserve policy, tolerance bands, governance responsibilities and a review calendar. Those controls are outside v1 and remain conditions for further work.
