# Harbourstone Foundation investment memo

## Recommendation

Use the diversified policy portfolio as the provisional strategic comparator for further due diligence: 35% global equities, 10% UK equities, 15% UK gilts, 20% GBP-hedged global aggregate bonds, 10% sterling corporate bonds, 7% physical gold and 3% broad commodities.

The allocation satisfies the coded mandate, retains all seven sleeves and balances growth, fixed income and real assets. It is not ready for implementation. Live market data, CPI history, cost assumptions and governance arrangements must be added before an investment committee could rely on the results.

## Mandate fit

Harbourstone is a fictional £100 million UK charitable foundation seeking CPI +3% annualised over rolling five-year periods while spending about 3% each year. The portfolio must remain long only and unlevered, hold at least 30% fixed income, and keep each asset at or below 40%.

The diversified policy portfolio is fully invested, long only and holds 45% fixed income. Its largest allocation is 35%. Ten per cent in gold and broad commodities adds a different response to inflation and crisis conditions, though these instruments introduce commodity, currency and product-structure risks.

## Evidence from the reproducible demonstration

The committed evidence uses deterministic synthetic returns from 2 January 2018 to 22 September 2026. It tests the workflow; it does not estimate future performance.

The policy portfolio produced a 3.9% demonstration CAGR, 8.0% annualised volatility, a 0.48 Sharpe ratio and a -15.4% maximum drawdown. The maximum-Sharpe portfolio showed stronger demonstration efficiency and a smaller drawdown, but it held only VWRL, IGLT and AGBP. That concentration is inconsistent with the committee's preference for multiple sources of return and demonstrates why an optimiser output should not become the recommendation automatically.

Risk is not equally distributed. Global and UK equities generated about 82% of the policy portfolio's covariance-based volatility contribution even though their combined weight was 45%. Equal capital weights therefore do not imply equal risk.

Under the hypothetical growth shock, the policy portfolio lost 10.0%, compared with 13.8% for the 60/40 reference. Its inflation-shock loss was 8.3%, and its rates-and-credit-shock loss was 9.6%. These are weighted assumptions rather than forecasts, but they show how the real-asset sleeves change the portfolio's response.

## Alternatives

The 60/40 reference is transparent and returned more in the demonstration, but it carried higher volatility, a larger drawdown and no direct real-asset allocation. The equal-weight portfolio spread capital evenly but produced the weakest growth and the largest drawdown in the synthetic sample.

The minimum-variance allocation reduced volatility to 5.6% but concentrated 76.2% in IGLT and AGBP. The maximum-Sharpe allocation also concentrated into three sleeves. Both results are useful sensitivity checks; neither is a complete client solution.

## Conditions before implementation

The committee should require a validated live-data run, a CPI benchmark series, product-cost and trading assumptions, an explicit cash reserve for one year of grants, and an implementation policy covering tolerance bands and oversight. The analysis should then be repeated with rolling and out-of-sample tests.

## Principal limitations

Yahoo Finance rate-limited the live validation attempt on 23 September 2026. The current figures are synthetic demonstrations. The model omits CPI history, liabilities, withdrawals, external fees, tax, spreads, market impact and stressed liquidity. Currency effects are embedded rather than decomposed. Optimisation is in-sample, and the linear stress tests do not model changing correlations or nonlinear payoffs.

## Decision

Advance the diversified policy portfolio to the next due-diligence stage. Do not treat the demonstration results as evidence that the portfolio will achieve CPI +3% or remain within the historical drawdown reference.
